You’ll start to notice as your puppy grows to adulthood and beyond that, just like humans, their body starts to slow down and wear out. Once that starts happening, pet owners know that the senior years have started. It may start with them not wanting to go out and play as much or needing more things to keep them healthy, but that time will come.

If your pet has become a senior dog, you are, of course, going to want to do everything you can to keep him healthy and help him thrive way into his older years. That starts with getting him the best senior dog food on the market today.

If you are unsure what the best senior food is, join us as we delve into our reviews of what we think are the best 11 senior dog foods for this year. Once we give you our choices, we will also give you a few tips on how to choose the best for your older furry friend as well.

    As dogs age, they often become pickier eaters and may struggle with digestive issues. The Farmer's Dog addresses both concerns with fresh, gently cooked meals that are easier on senior dog digestive systems than heavily processed kibble.

    These veterinary nutritionist-developed recipes use human-grade ingredients and real meat proteins that are gentle on aging stomachs. The fresh format provides natural hydration, which is especially important for senior dogs who may need help staying hydrated. The soft texture is also ideal for dogs with dental issues or missing teeth.

    What makes this particularly good for seniors is the customization. You can tailor the recipes to your dog's changing needs as they age, adjusting for weight management, reduced activity levels, or specific health concerns. Each meal is pre-portioned to prevent overfeeding, which is crucial for less-active senior dogs.

    While fresh food does cost more than dry kibble, many owners find the improved energy levels, healthier weight, and better digestion worth the investment for their senior companions. With all of this in mind, we think that The Farmer’s Dog is the best dog food for senior dogs.

    Buyer's Guide: Selecting the Best Senior Dog Food

    That’s our top 11 reviews of what we consider the best senior dog food on the market. But, of course, now that your dog is in his golden age of adulthood, you want to give him only the healthiest food that’s age-appropriate. If you are unsure how to choose the best for your pet, we’ll give you a few tips in our section below to help you decide.

    High-Protein, Low-Fat

    While you’ve always tried to give your dog the best food with high protein and low-fat ingredients, it’s even more important now that he’s a senior. In addition, your dog has slowed down and doesn’t get around as much, so it’s important to choose a food that’ll give him his protein but help him maintain his optimal weight at the same time.

    Strike a Balance Between Canned and Dry

    Some canines lose weight and muscle mass as they age, so you want to strike a balance between canned food and dry food in this case. Remember, dogs can get picky as they age, so canned food might be something that whets your dog’s appetite when dry food doesn’t.

    Skip Artificial Ingredients

    Artificial ingredients aren’t good for anyone, human or animal, but they are especially bad for the elderly. You can help your dog maintain a healthy older lifestyle by choosing foods with no artificial flavors, ingredients, or preservatives.

    The Health of Your Dog is Key

    You already know that every dog is different, so your pet's health going into his senior years is also essential. For example, if your dog suffers from joint pain, a sensitive stomach, or skin problems as he ages, you’ll want a different senior dog food than you would if he was completely healthy.

    Vet Recommendations

    If you’re having a problem figuring out the best food to feed your senior dog, then it’s best to take him to your vet for a checkup. Not only should you look for foods that have vet recommended on the label, but you should also take your vet's advice into account as well. They’ve been doing this for a long time, so their advice is the best out there.

    We all love our pets like their family and don’t want to think of them entering their twilight years. However, it happens to the best of us, so preparing to deal with it ahead of time is key. Choose the food that you think will keep your senior dog healthy, happy, and moving around fine for at least a few more years to come.
