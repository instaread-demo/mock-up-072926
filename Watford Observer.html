Where sinkholes have opened in and around Watford this year

28th July

Watford

By Liam Fitzpatrick Reporter

The first half of 2026 has seen Watford plagued by sinkholes with several roads being forced to shut.

A combination of bad weather and Hertfordshire's vulnerability to voids has made it a particularly challenging year for the county's motorists.

Hertfordshire County Council has said that the ground in the county is particularly vulnerable to water damage because it is mostly clay and chalk.

Chalk dissolves in water and in a hot dry summer clay soil dries out and becomes powdery. This means any water escaping from a damaged pipe can easily wash it away, creating a hollow space underground.

The Watford Observer has rounded up several of the sinkholes that have appeared in the area this year:

Hagden Lane (THREE times)

The sinkhole in Hagden Lane, Watford, after it was excavated (Image: Hertfordshire County Council)

This key West Watford road has been blocked by a sinkhole THREE times, all in the early part of the year.

It was reported that the road “noticeably sunk” on January 21, sparking investigations beneath the surface which discovered “no further issues”.

The sinkhole was filled but the road surface failed again on February 9 and was shut for two more days before a third void appeared the following month.

Aldenham Road

The sinkhole in Aldenham Road, Bushey (Image: Jake Phillips)

The most recent sinkhole came in Aldenham Road in Bushey.

The void opened up on July 23 near the traffic lights by Ye Corner and led to a two-day road closure while Affinity Water resolved the issue.

Oxhey Road

Depression in the road surface in Oxhey Road (Image: Hertfordshire County Council)

This busy Watford junction was closed earlier this month after a sinkhole depression in the road surface was reported.

This caused traffic woes at the busy crossroad junction of Eastbury Road, Deacons Hill, and Oxhey Road.

Essex Lane

Two sinkholes appeared within hours of each other (Image: Google Maps/Canva)

This sinkhole came on the same day as the first Hagden Lane sinkhole on a frustrating January afternoon for the town's motorists.

The two roads are just five minutes away from each other by car.

Hayling Road

This South Oxhey sinkhole required specialist equipment and took weeks to fix.

The 'sinkhole' in Hayling Road, South Oxhey (Image: Cllr Steve Cox)

Motorists had reported a “big dip” in February before Hertfordshire County Council (HCC) had to draft in an excavator, which was larger than the normal mini digger at the depot, to safely break open the mouth of the hole at the junction with Ralston Way.

Thames Water was twice forced to extend a road closure permit while it worked on the large hole.

Baldwins Lane

This busy road in Croxley Green was forced shut at the end of May because of what passers-by described as a clear "hole in the road".

The 320 and 322 bus routes were sent on diversion, with the road blocked off between the Harvester and Spar.
