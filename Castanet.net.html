No missing-person report or abandoned vehicle in connection with reported drowning

No sign of missing swimmer

Tim Petruk - Jul 28, 2026 / 10:00 am | Story: 626202
Photo: Michael Potestio
FILE - Emergency crews on the beach at Riverside Park shortly before 7:30 p.m. on Friday, July 24, 2026, for a reported drowning in the Thompson River.

Crews have scoured the Thompson River with kayaks, sonar and RCMP divers after witnesses reported seeing a person go under on Friday night.

Kamloops Search and Rescue said there is no sign of a person reported to have drowned in the Thompson River near Riverside Park.

Emergency crews were called to the area at about 7:30 p.m. on Friday. They searched the area on Friday night and KSAR teams were mobilized Saturday.

“We have several witnesses that observed somebody going under just outside of the swim area — they saw arms up in the air,” KSAR search manager Alan Hobler told Castanet.

“There’s no missing-person report, there’s no bags or equipment on the beach and there’s no vehicles in the parking lot unaccounted for. It’s a little bit of a mystery who this person was, but I think we all believe the witnesses are correct — they did see what they said they saw.”

Hobler said KSAR searchers concentrated first on what they considered the most likely spot.

“Usually when people drown, they sink and then just go right to the bottom and stay there,” he said.

“So we searched that and probed it with the kayaks, and we have a boat with side-scan sonar that also came in and searched that area, but didn’t locate any targets specifically there.”

A police dive team was in the river on Saturday, but they could not find anything. Mounties also flew drones above the river to look for any sign of the person.

The active search was suspended after Saturday’s efforts, with follow-up searches planned. Hobler said KSAR could be back out on the river next weekend.

Searchers will also be watching for another missing person believed to be in the river following a crash in May on Schubert Drive along the North Thompson River.
