    BIKE CHALLENGE

    Clinton residents participate in this year's Pan-Mass Challenge

    CLINTON — Robert and Harry Knight, a father and son from Clinton, are participating in the 47th Pan-Mass Challenge (PMC) for Dana-Farber, an annual bike-a-thon taking place Aug. 1-2 across Massachusetts that raises money for cancer research at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute. They are among more than 6,000 cyclists and 4,000 volunteers participating in the event, which Robert says, raises more money for charity than any other athletic fundraiser in the country.

    This year marks Robert’s 24th PMC ride and Harry’s sixth.

    “In the early 2000s, my friend who lived in Boston said, ‘Let’s do this ride together.’ Now we’ve been doing it every year as a mini reunion over the summer for a group of friends to hang out together and do something for a great cause,” Robert said.

    “Now our kids are involved, including my son, and it’s been a great experience to do something with him and to spend seven hours on a bike together.”

    Billy Starr, founder and chairman of the Pan-Mass Challenge, started the bike-a-thon in 1980. While the ride traditionally begins in Sturbridge, Massachusetts, the 2026 event marks the first year riders will start at the College of the Holy Cross in Worcester.

    The PMC offers 14 route options ranging from 25 to 177 miles. Riders may participate on Saturday, Sunday or both days. On Saturday, riders begin in either Wellesley or Worcester and finish in Bourne. On Sunday, riders start in either Bourne or Wellesley and finish in Provincetown or Wellesley.

    Some teams and individual riders also participate in an independent “Day Zero” ride, cycling from the New York border to the official start line the day before the event begins.

    Over the years, Robert has raised $155,709 for Dana-Farber Cancer Institute. Riders commit to raising a minimum amount based on their route. This year, Robert’s fundraising minimum was $6,000.

    “This is a really well run and incredibly efficient organization. Every dollar raised goes directly to the cancer center,” he said. “It’s a very uplifting experience. From the start to the end, there’s always people cheering you on, yelling, and passing water out.”

    The PMC’s Pedal Partner program connects cancer patients with teams participating in the bike-a-thon. Riders often meet patients at water stations along their routes, a reminder of what their fundraising supports.

    “It’s probably my favorite weekend of the year. It’s just so powerful seeing everyone who’s involved with the cause and you meet a lot of different people on the ride,” Harry said. “Half of them are cancer survivors or going through treatment, or half of them are riding because people are impacted by it. It’s so powerful meeting different people and hearing their stories.”

    “It’s a great cause and a good way to reconnect with friends and family,” Robert said.
