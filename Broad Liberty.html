Delaware County’s unusual form of county government has become the subject of debate lately. As fumbles, scandals, tax hikes, and general mismanagement piles up, voters across Delco are asking why — unlike the other counties in the region — they are living in a one-party system, with no minority party check on the processes of government. 

Now, some citizens are starting to wake up to the disaster of party machine government. The Delaware County Democratic Party, naturally, wants you to believe that more oversight and openness are useless at best, and nasty Republican tricks at worst.

That is, at least, the message they are putting out in a video statement by Cheryl Grasso that was posted on the party’s Facebook page this week. Grasso is the new chair of the Delaware County Democrats. 

The attempt to restore minority party seats to county council, Grasso says, would make county government “bigger [and] more expensive, without delivering any clear benefit to the Delaware County benefits.”

Those of you familiar with Delco Democrats may need some clarification: she’s saying they’re against it for this reason.

She goes on to say that minority party representation is a relic of the past, that government is now so honest and transparent that no one needs to keep an eye on them — they have mandatory disclosures and public meetings now! Like the public disclosure that should have alerted voters to Council President Richard Womack’s tax delinquencies before his reelection, not after it? Or the public records of the meetings of the budget task force that remained locked away — again, until an election had passed? Or the Executive Director’s annual budget proposal that is no longer available online and can only be obtained via in person inspection at the County Clerk’s office? 

Grasso says the people chose their government, but what kind of choice is it when facts are concealed until the votes are safely counted? A minority party member would have pushed for transparency before the election. 

So much for the majority keeping an eye on itself. As the ancient Romans were wont to ask, quis custodiet ipsos custodes? Who watches the watchers?

Even Grasso’s attempt to frame this as a Republican about-face confuses the history of the county: when home rule and the five-member at-large council were approved in May 1975, Republicans did indeed hold two of the three seats on the county commission. But Democrats had made massive gains in the state elections in Delco in 1974, and expected to win again in 1975 as the old Republican machine crumbled in the face of a national backlash to Watergate. Dems saw victory ahead and wanted to lock in complete control. Even the Philadelphia Inquirer endorsed the home rule charter change.

Instead, a new-look Republican Party squeaked out a victory in 1975 and again for the two new seats in 1977. The current county council arrangement was not the sneaky Republican trick they would have you believe. And, more to the point, who cares which party supported it in 1975? In a republic, voters are meant to decide ideas based on their merit, not based on ad hominem arguments and appeals to tribalism — much less appeals to the tribes of fifty years ago.

Every other county in the region — and 60 out of 67 in the state — has three county commissioners, two from the majority party, one from the minority. It works fine. It does not even hinder the majority from enacting the agenda that the voters endorsed by electing them. The two Democratic commissioners in Montgomery County, Bucks County, and Chester County are not obstructed in the least by the single Republican on each of those commissions — but those Republicans do ensure that business is conducted in public, not behind closed doors in one-party war rooms. Even Philadelphia has three non-Democrats on City Council and a Republican on their vestigial county commission. It’s still a Democratic town.

The final, farcical argument is that having seven councilmembers would expand government costs. This from a party that has imposed the largest increases in taxes in the county’s modern history. If the Democrats are that concerned with the taxpayer’s dollar, they should agree to reduce the salaries by distributing the same amount that now goes to five to the seven that the reformers would have replace them.

Don’t hold your breath on that one. 

But there’s another way to look at this: How much would two watchdogs on a council that has raised taxes by nearly 55 percent in three years potentially save the taxpayer by exposing wasteful spending? 

No, what the Delco Democrats really fear is what all one-party governments fear: public oversight. And according to this latest video, not only are they against it, but they don’t even want to let the voters have their say on the question. So much for the stalwart defenders of democracy.
