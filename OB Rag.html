Taking Notions from a Book to Heart After a Tragedy

by Ernie McCray

With the tragedy
at the mosque
still on my mind
I’ve been thinking
of a wonderful book
I just finished,
“The Future is Peace,”
written by Aziz Abu Sarah,
a Palestinian,
and Maoz Inon,
an Israeli,
who, rather than focusing on their ethnicities,
choose to view themselves
as human beings
who are trying to cultivate

a culture of peace

in a world that would

expect them to be enemies

taking into consideration

that both of them

have lost family to the conflict

between their people.

 

But they choose love,

peace,

and reconciliation

and their story

resonated with me

deeply,

especially a passage

that highlighted

that there

is a mirror between

Israel and Palestine

wherein each side

reflects their own rage

back at themselves,

a mirror that must be broken

so that

the opposing parties

can look one another in the eye

to humanize each other

and even more importantly

come to recognize

the humanity

in all concerned

and the book declares that

“The first step on the path to peace

is listening to the other side’s story.”

 

I can’t help but agree

although such an act

in our global society

would be so hard

to bring to fruition

but Aziz and Maoz

stress that in the listening

there can evolve

cooperation between people based

on equality and respect,

that shared purpose

can overcome inherited suspicion,

and that peace is not only something

to talk about –

it is something to build,

day by day,

through real friendships

and equal partnerships.

 

Oh, it seems

that in a world

where establishing such common ground

is pursued

there wouldn’t be much of a likelihood

of two teenage boys

being so absolutely hateful and intolerant

of their fellow human beings

and different perspectives

that they would set out to take their lives

and their own lives.

 

With all I’ve said

I hope this tragedy

awakens San Diegans

to the reality

that we have a duty

to use this tragedy

as a catalyst

for making our city better

when it comes to human relations

and along such lines

Taha Hassane, the Director of the Islamic Center,

says:

“All of us are responsible

for spreading

the culture of tolerance,

the culture of love.”

 

Ameen

and as a start

we must

take such sentiment

to heart.
