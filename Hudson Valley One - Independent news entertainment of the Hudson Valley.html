Environmental justice questions continue to delay Zena Homes development

by Nick Henderson
July 22, 2026
in Development, Politics & Government
0

0:00 / 4:15
Instaread

The town of Ulster Planning Board appears poised to determine that the proposed 30-lot Zena Homes subdivision will have no significant environmental impact, while the ZBA is reviewing a challenge to a determination that the project does not warrant town board action.

Environmental justice-related changes to the required environmental assessment form pushed back the decision until August.

The new form asks whether the development is within the 500-year floodplain, where the previous version was limited to the 100-year floodplain. It is in neither.

New questions extend possible impacts to include climate instead of being limited to air.

Another question asks whether the development will have an impact on a disadvantaged community based on certain economic parameters. The town of Ulster is considered a disadvantaged community, but the area of the development is not, planning consultant Max Stach argued.

“So now going forward, whenever we have an application in the town of Ulster, we’re going to have to make this determination on whether the action is going to unfairly impact overburdened disadvantaged communities,” he said.

Stach recommended distributing the updated form with the responses to allow feedback.

Woodstock Land Conservancy attorney Cassondra Britton sent a letter to the Planning Board July 14 continuing to argue for a positive declaration, meaning the project will cause significant environmental impact.

She argues the new EAF requires an assessment of cumulative pollution burdens and indirect effects, given Ulster is a disadvantaged community. She also argues for consideration of environmental justice issues.

Further, she notes Eastwoods Drive through the town of Woodstock, the sole access to the development, fails to comply with Woodstock town code and poses public safety issues due to its lack of capacity for emergency access.

Meanwhile, the Zoning Board of Appeals heard arguments on whether the Ulster Town Board should have jurisdiction, given the development’s size and scope. Britton has asked the town of Ulster Planning Board to hold any decision in abeyance until the ZBA makes a decision.

Woodstock Land Conservancy had sued the town, arguing it had failed to provide an interpretation of town law that allows the town board to review any project exceeding 2,500 square feet. The Ulster County Supreme Court issued an injunction requiring an interpretation.

Building Inspector William Tutt then determined no single structure meets the threshold, so it should stay within the Planning Board.

However, WLC’s attorney, Cassondra Britton, said the Planning Board voted May 13, 2025, to refer the project to the Town Board and never reversed that decision.

Britton warned that if Tutt’s ruling stands, any developer of a large-scale project could restructure it as a series of individual components to avoid town review. She further argued this is segmentation under the State Environmental Quality Review Act, or SEQRA, which is the breaking up of a project into smaller parts to evade review.

“The planning board has neither the mandate nor the tools to adequately weigh the impacts of a project of this size and scale on lands that have been designated for protection by the town itself,” said WLC Executive Director Andy Mossey. “The town board is the appropriate body for review.”

WLC’s 210-acre Israel Whitman Sanctuary borders the proposed development.

Initially, the ZBA opened a public hearing at its July 13 meeting but soon closed it. After objections from the audience about a lack of a public hearing on the agenda, the ZBA agreed to keep written comments open until its Aug. 12 meeting.

ZBA Chairman Kevin Reginato was in favor of holding the public hearing but limiting comments to the scope of determining whether Tutt’s interpretation was correct.

Town attorney Jason Kovacs cautioned that would be difficult.

“Although you of course want the scope to be the very narrow issue before the board, I think you’re going to have a hard time cutting people off who would want to talk about other aspects of the project, which are legitimate aspects … the SEQRA process, the road, everything else, which is certainly important information but outside the narrow scope of what this board is charged with.”

Written comments can be sent to the town of Ulster ZBA by emailing planningzoning@townofulster.ny.gov or sending a letter to 584 East Chester St., Kingston, NY 12401.

Nick Henderson was raised in Woodstock starting at the age of three and attended Onteora schools, then SUNY New Paltz after spending a year at SUNY Potsdam under the misguided belief he would become a music teacher.
