Abigail Kirsch unveils the first phase of reimagined event spaces at Tappan Hill Mansion

by Georgette Gouveia
July 27, 2026
0

Abigail Kirsch celebrated the completed first phase of a two-year renovation project at Tappan Hill Mansion in Tarrytown Wednesday, July 22, with the kind of event it does so well — a lavish, scrumptious breakfast, served by a gracious wait staff, followed by opening remarks and tours of the landmarked, 10-acre, 30,000-square-foot property, whose storied past features 19th-century humorist and author Mark Twain.

The renovated spaces include The Promenade, which serves as a cocktail reception area for weddings, and the Pavilion Ballroom, for wedding dinners or indoor weddings in case of inclement weather. (The Promenade actually bridges the Pavilion Ballroom and the Pavilion Terrace, used for outdoor weddings.)

Sales manager Doug Dator, who led our tour, said that weddings make up 65% of the 125 events Tappan Hill does each year. The rest include bar and bat mitzvahs, galas, fundraisers, baptisms, bridal and baby showers, corporate holiday parties and meetings and memorial services. 

Updated decorative wall trim and lounge seating areas, dramatic ceiling drapery, sconces and chandeliers and redesigned architectural details characterize phase one.

In phase two, Tappan Hill will turn its attention to carpeting and the refurbishment of the Hudson Ballroom, which along with Hudson Garden serves as a smaller event/wedding space. (The Pavilion Ballroom can seat 225 to 325 people, while the Hudson Ballroom holds 150, Dator said.)

The Skyline Suite, located up the sweeping staircase on the second floor and often used for wedding after-parties, is part of phase two as well. (The second floor also holds two “man caves” for grooms and groomsmen to get ready and/or relax and two rooms for brides and bridesmaids.)

The renovations are intended to marry Tappan Hill’s timeless stone architecture with modern design, said Danielle Shapiro, regional director of catering, who added that the company does not disclose renovation costs.

Tappan Hill has a rich cultural history. It begins with Samuel Clemens, better known as Mark Twain (“The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn,” “The Adventures of Tom Sawyer”), who purchased the Hillcrest Estate, on which Tappan Hill now sits, in 1902, intending it for his daughter Jean, according to a marker outside the property’s entrance. (It had been built in 1882 by Capt. William Casey.)

Historical accounts vary as to how much time Twain spent in Tarrytown, if any at all. What sources do agree on is that Twain sold the property in 1904 to lawyer Charles Gardner, who in turn sold it in 1915 to stockbroker Jacques Halle. He demolished Hillcrest to make way for a new manse he named Halleston. It was sold in 1941 to developer David Swope, who rechristened it Tappan Hill Mansion.

Enter Abigail Kirsch — chef, author and hospitality pioneer — who started a cooking school in the 1960s in the basement of her Chappaqua home, with husband Bob and son Jim eventually joining what became a catering and events company. In 1989, the Kirsches took over and restored Tappan Hill as the exclusive headquarters for catering. In October 2016, Jim Kirsch sold the business to Elior Group SA, a French-based multinational catering and food services corporation that retained the Kirsch name.

Besides Tappan Hill Mansion, Abigail Kirsch owns and operates The Loading Dock in Stamford while doing off-premises catering at prominent sites around the metro area. Catering brings in $6 million annually, Dator said, while off-premises catering revenues are $10 million a year.

A non-wedding event at Tappan Hill Mansion costs $100 to $175 a person (plus tax); a wedding, $285 to $295 per person (plus tax).
